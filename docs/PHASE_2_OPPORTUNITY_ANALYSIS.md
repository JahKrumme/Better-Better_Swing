## What could be valuable, if the gaps are resolved?

The strongest recurring research leads are **High 5, Stake.us and RealPrize**, whose rules actually describe free promotional-currency acquisition. Sportzino and Zula add free reward leads but impose unpublished balance/bonus conditions. No fixed, conflict-free recurring amount met the complete standard. There is consequently no defensible per-claim, weekly or monthly promotional total to report.

Sportzino's registration table documents **1 SC** and Zula's documents **2 SC**, but these are unverified opportunity leads, not an immediately collectible 3 SC total. Their larger task packages involve identity/phone or social connections and marketing consent; the first daily reward is included in their package totals. Counting both the whole welcome package and the daily reward would double-count value. Sportzino's conflicting daily table would arithmetically sum to 5.3 SC/week, but projecting that as 22.7 SC/30 days would hide the conflicting 1 SC Day 1 claim and undisclosed balance threshold. **Neither calculation is an opportunity estimate.**

High 5, Stake.us, Sportzino, Zula and RealPrize describe prize-redemption paths. Those are conditional paths after promotional play, verification and minimum balances. An official redemption ratio is not an expected return: loss during playthrough, assigned multipliers, fees and balance accumulation still matter. No game-specific conservative return model or achieved redemption was verified, so estimated cash remains UNKNOWN. Postal requests are outside the user's $0 requirement even when advertised as “no purchase necessary.”

## Product-owner answers

1. **Useful enough today?** No verified collectible plan yet. Research organization has value; the earning hypothesis remains unproven.
2. **Enough recurring value for monitoring?** Not established. First resolve one reproducible daily reward with a known amount and usable prize path. Automated website monitoring also needs a policy assessment; DraftKings expressly prohibits automated collection/access.
3. **Manual time?** No observed claiming session was timed. A planning assumption of 1–3 minutes per ordinary login/claim would mean 5–15 minutes/day for five future qualifying platforms, excluding onboarding, KYC, promotional play and redemption. This is not a measured estimate or a current collection plan. Those additional tasks could dominate the time cost.
4. **Any explicit automatic-claim permission?** None.
5. **How many explicit prohibitions?** Nine platforms have relevant automation restrictions. Stake expressly prohibits acquiring Stake Cash with automated systems; PrizePicks prohibits unauthorized automated prize accumulation. High 5 expressly prohibits automated play/redemption, DraftKings automated website collection/access, Caesars session bots; Pulsz, McLuck, Hello Millions and RealPrize prohibit game-play bots. Do not represent those narrower clauses as nine explicit daily-click prohibitions. Zula's purchase/transaction clause establishes no free-claim permission.
6. **Dashboard plus reminders useful if claiming stays manual?** Potentially: it could consolidate rules, costs, reset windows and redemption progress. Meaningful practical value requires quantified, qualified recurring rewards first. This run adds evidence to the existing dashboard but builds no reminders.
7. **Proceed to Phase 3?** **No.** Resolve the strongest accessible leads and this user's age eligibility, then compare achievable promotional value, expected losses and actual manual effort. Automatic claiming is unjustified without affirmative permission; no such permission was found.
8. **Must all four blocked brands be resolved first?** No. They could improve coverage, but there is no evidence that they rescue the opportunity. Prioritize accessible High 5/Stake/RealPrize reward specifics and the linked document/help gaps. Investigate the four later without assuming favorable rewards or permissions.
